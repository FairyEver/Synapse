# Enforce problem-feedback privacy in fail-closed layers

Problem feedback uses layered, fail-closed privacy checks: the built-in Synapse Skill first performs semantic review, then the MCP boundary and server independently enforce one authoritative deterministic policy and shared test corpus. A match rejects the exact draft instead of silently redacting it, exposes only the risk category, and requires a newly displayed and confirmed draft; rejected bodies and fragments must not enter responses, logs, or audit records. This is a defense-in-depth commitment rather than a claim that automated rules can identify every sensitive fact in arbitrary natural language, so the user must still review the complete confirmation draft.

## Prohibited content

- Real authentication and secret material is prohibited, including passwords, PINs, verification and recovery codes, API keys, access or refresh tokens, session tokens, cookies, authorization values, private keys, seed phrases, credential-bearing connection strings or URLs, temporary credentials, and hashes, fingerprints, or other derivatives that can be associated with those secret values. Non-secret names, risk categories, and explicit redaction placeholders remain allowed. The policy must distinguish secret-derived identifiers from ordinary file hashes, public error codes, and other non-secret diagnostic identifiers rather than rejecting every identifier-shaped value.
- Raw local paths are prohibited whether absolute, home-relative, drive-based, UNC, `file://`, embedded in a shell prompt or stack trace, or relative in a way that exposes a user, organization, customer, or project-specific name. Only deliberately abstracted logical paths composed from the fixed placeholders, such as `<home>/<project>/<file>` or `<project>/<module>/<file>`, are allowed. Semantic review owns ambiguous relative-path judgment; deterministic checks must not use high-false-positive patterns or claim that they can recognize every relative path.
- Personally or organizationally identifying content is prohibited, including contact details, account and social identities, government identifiers, organization and customer names, internal project or unreleased product names, host and device identifiers, network addresses and internal domains, and user, team, session, or device IDs. V1 accepts no contact details even when voluntarily offered because it has no reply mechanism. Non-identifying general environment facts, Synapse itself, public third-party product or dependency names, public versions, and public error codes remain allowed.
- Raw user work and business data is prohibited, including project source, configuration or prompt bodies, document content, database rows and business values, full terminal output, logs or stack traces, Agent conversations, tool-call records, clipboard and file content, and real Workflow inputs or outputs. Feedback describes the problem rather than serving as an evidence bundle. Allowed context is limited to synthetic minimal reproductions, necessary short error excerpts that have passed review, abstract scenario descriptions, and directly relevant public Synapse identifiers or short instruction excerpts.
- URLs are allowed only when directly relevant to reproduction and are stable, public, credential-free official product or documentation URLs. URLs with query strings or fragments, temporary or signed URLs, local or internal locations, private resources, identity-bearing paths, and URLs of uncertain public status are prohibited and must be abstracted; unnecessary URLs are omitted even when public.
- Exact occurrence times, time zones or locale settings, and request, trace, correlation, installation, crash, or other unique identifiers that can be used to associate a report with a user are prohibited in the body. Coarse order, frequency, and relative timing remain allowed, as do public error numbers that do not identify a specific request. Server-generated receipt time and record identity, if retained, are operational fields outside the body.
